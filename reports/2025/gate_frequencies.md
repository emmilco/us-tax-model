## Notes by situation

- `home_or_charity`: About 65% of households own their home and nearly all pay property tax (Census, recalled); renters who give to charity add more. Itemizers are only 9.4% of returns, which measures who deducts, not who would answer yes. The microdata's imputed mortgage interest, property tax and charity flags (19%, 22%, 20%) follow itemizers and were not used.
- `charity`: About half of US households give to charity in a year (Philanthropy Panel Study, recalled); per return, with many single young filers, about 40%. Itemized deductions (9.4% of returns) measure who deducts gifts, not who gives.
- `state_refund`: Only refunds that are taxable are counted (1.9% of returns), but everyone who received a refund answers yes. Most of the about 85% of filers in states with an income tax file a state return, and most state returns end in a refund, so about 40%.
- `married`: Joint (with qualifying surviving spouses) plus separate returns. Married people filing as head of household (living apart) answer yes too, so the share is a floor.
- `workplace_plan`: About half of private-sector workers participate in an employer plan (BLS National Compensation Survey, recalled) and nearly all public employees; with W-2 wages on 79% of returns that is roughly 35% of returns. The microdata's 401(k) contribution variables flag 30% but identically for traditional, Roth and IRA contributions, so they were not used. Each W-2's box 12 question (w2.box12_any) is yes for still more returns, since code DD (employer health coverage) is common.
- `interest`: The larger of taxable and tax-exempt interest; the high end adds them.
- `ira_owner`: About 4 in 10 US households own an IRA (ICI, recalled); per return, with single filers, about a third.
- `dependents`: The child and other dependent credit alone (nonrefundable part) is on 24.3% of returns, a floor.
- `business_any`: Each part raked to its IRS count; the ladder also asks "in any earlier year" (carryovers), which adds a little.
- `retirement_distributions`: IRS counts bound the union from 20.3% (pensions alone) to 30.7% (summed). Rollovers and conversions reported on a Form 1099-R also answer yes and are partly outside both counts.
- `community_property`: Arizona, California, Idaho, Louisiana, Nevada, New Mexico, Texas, Washington and Wisconsin. Asked of everyone today though it matters only for married filers and head of household. By resident population these states hold about 31% of the US (Census, recalled), above the microdata's 23%; the range spans both.
- `dividends`: The high end adds capital gain distributions reported directly on Form 1040.
- `age65`: Age of the primary taxpayer only (the table's note 1); a joint return with only the spouse 65 or older adds a little.
- `self_employment`: Net income and net loss are disjoint, so their sum counts returns with a Schedule C.
- `noncash_gifts`: Donating used goods is common; perhaps a third of those who give also give goods. The roster is asked of everyone today (survey 2.2).
- `overtime`: New for 2025, so no IRS count. The microdata's overtime premium is imputed for hourly non-exempt workers; the annual share of workers with any overtime is uncertain.
- `securities_sales`: Returns with a net long-term gain or loss from sales of capital assets (disjoint columns, summed) are 11.4%, a floor on returns with any sale; all returns with Schedule D gain or loss are 16.3%, but that includes returns with only distributions or a carryover. Sales with no net gain or loss, and a home sale whose gain is excluded, also answer yes.
- `scope_schedule1`: The union of the coverage estimate's categories behind this screen, each raked to its IRS count; NOL and Form 4797 (no microdata variable, 0.6% and 1.1% alone) are left out, so the share is a floor.
- `hsa`: Only own-contribution deductions (1.25%) and taxable distributions (0.29%) are counted; employer contributions and qualified distributions also require Form 8889. About 37 million HSA accounts exist (Devenir, recalled), most with a contribution or distribution in a year, held by perhaps 15 to 20 million returns, about 11%. Weak and across the cut. The archived tables count only a small part of Form 8889 filers; a count of Forms 8889 (not archived) would settle it.
- `schedule_e`: Rental and royalty returns are 5.9%, partnership 3.3%, S corporation 3.5%, estate and trust 0.4%; summed 13.2%. They overlap (the same filers often hold several), so about 10%.
- `education`: Education credits are claimed on 4.5% of returns (the larger of the nonrefundable education and American opportunity counts), but expenses paid without a credit (income over the limit, scholarships covering tuition, students filing their own return while a parent claims them) also answer yes. About 19 million students in postsecondary education (NCES, recalled) touch roughly 14 million returns, about 9%. The microdata's qualified_tuition_expenses flags 0.7% and was not used. Weak and across the cut.
- `marketplace`: Returns with advance premium tax credit were 6.0% in 2023. Marketplace enrolment rose from about 16 million (2023) to about 24 million (2025) under the enhanced credits (CMS, recalled), so about 8.5% for 2025.
- `ira_contributions`: Deducted traditional IRA contributions are 1.6% of returns; nondeductible traditional and Roth contributions appear on no counted line. About one in ten to one in seven US households contributes to an IRA in a year (ICI surveys, recalled), and households are fewer than returns, so about 8%. The microdata's IRA contribution variables flag 30% of filing units, the same units as its 401(k) variables, and were not used. For person.sav_trad alone (traditional IRA only) the share is lower, about 3%.
- `schedule1_line8`: Other income (net income plus net loss, line 8z and others) is on 4.8% of returns, gambling 1.4%, cancelled debt 0.24%; summed 6.5%. Honest yes adds gambling winnings and jury pay that go unreported, so about 8%. Across the cut at its high end.
- `student_loan_interest`: The deduction was on 4.3% of returns in 2023, when federal loan payments had resumed only in October after the pause. Before the pause it was on about 12 million returns (recalled), about 7.5%; borrowers above the income limit also answer yes, so about 8% for 2025. Weak and across the cut; the 2023 count is depressed by the payment pause.
- `foreign_tax`: Returns claiming the credit; holders of international funds who do not claim it, and itemizers who deduct foreign tax instead, also answer yes.
- `estimated_payments`: Estimated payments alone; the high end adds payments with an extension (1.5%), which the ladder's question also covers.
- `savings_accounts`: About 16 million 529 accounts (ISS Market Intelligence, recalled), several per family in many cases, so perhaps 8 to 10 million owner returns; ABLE, Coverdell and Archer MSAs add little.
- `equity_comp_or_amt`: Employee stock purchase plan participants and holders of restricted stock units and options number perhaps 8 to 15 million (NCEO, recalled); AMT on an earlier return (0.09% in 2023, 0.07% with a prior-year minimum tax credit) adds little.
- `other_income_8z`: SOI's other income covers more of Schedule 1 line 8 than 8z alone, so this overstates the roster slightly; unreported small items understate it.
- `dependent_filer`: Returns marked as dependent returns. A filer who could be claimed but is not (and so does not mark it) also answers yes; the table cannot show them.
- `capital_loss_carryover`: Long-term carryovers are on 3.5% of returns and short-term on 3.2%; summed 6.7%. Many returns carry both, so about 4.5%.
- `care_expenses`: The child and dependent care credit is claimed on 3.6% of returns; filers who paid care but take the employer exclusion instead, or whose credit is zero, also answer yes.
- `noncitizen`: Noncitizens without lawful permanent residence (temporary visa holders, refugees and asylees awaiting adjustment, the undocumented) number about 15 to 20 million people (DHS and Census, recalled); returns filed with ITINs alone are about 4 million. About 3.5%.
- `car_loan`: About 16 million new vehicles are sold a year, perhaps 60% on a loan rather than a lease or cash and about half assembled in the US, so about 5 million personal-use purchases, about 3% of returns. A question asking about any car loan would draw far more yes answers; the microdata's household auto loan interest flags 32% and was not used. Wording-sensitive.
- `gambling`: Reported gambling earnings are on 1.4% of returns; legal sports betting and lotteries reach far more people, and any winnings answer yes, so about 3%.
- `home_energy`: Summed (overlap assumed small); 2025 is the last year for both credits, so a rush may raise it.
- `foreign_accounts`: About 1.5 million FBARs are filed a year (FinCEN, recalled), each for accounts over $10,000 in total; line 7a is yes at any balance, and many of the roughly 45 million foreign-born residents keep accounts in their country of origin. About 2.5%. Wide; below the cut at either end.
- `schedule_d_other_forms`: Each Schedule D other-form or pass-through column is at most 1.2% of returns and Form 4797 is 1.1%; summed 4.8%, with large overlap among pass-through owners.
- `casualty`: Deducted casualty losses are a few thousand returns (disaster losses 0.007%), but the question as worded draws yes from anyone with a damaged car or a burglary, perhaps several percent; scoped to federally declared disasters it is well under 1%. Wording-sensitive; below the cut at any reasonable wording.
- `household_employer`: Household employment tax (Schedule H) is on 0.15% of returns; household employers who should file but do not, and families with nannies or housekeepers who are not employees, answer yes too. A wording that includes any paid cleaner or contractor draws far more. Wording-sensitive.
- `tips`: New for 2025, so no IRS count. The microdata's tip income is imputed for tipped occupations; about 4 million workers in tipped occupations (Budget Lab, recalled) agrees in size.
- `schedule_b_special`: Each item arises only with directly held bonds, seller financing, nominee accounts or savings bonds redeemed for education; a small part of the 34% with interest.
- `scope_other_income`: The countable parts (other earned income, household employee wages not on a W-2, taxable dependent care benefits, tips not on a W-2, Form 8919 wages) are 0.8% at the largest and 1.4% summed; the screen lists further treatments with no count.
- `military`: About 1.3 million active-duty and 0.8 million reserve and Guard members (DoD, recalled), some on the same joint return; about 1.3% of returns. The microdata's is_military (0.5%) follows the CPS, which leaves out most active-duty members, and was not used.
- `business_carryovers`: Net operating loss returns alone are 0.63%; section 179 and QBI loss carryforwards have no count and mostly fall on the same business owners.
- `dependent_care_benefits`: Taxable dependent care benefits are on 0.27% of returns; most benefits are excluded and uncounted. Dependent care FSAs have roughly 1.5 to 2.5 million participants (recalled), about 1% of returns.
- `farm`: Schedule F returns (net income plus net loss, disjoint) are 1.09%; cooperative patrons without a farm and fishing on Schedule C add a little.
- `scope_credits`: Other nonrefundable credits (general business, adoption, clean vehicle, elderly, mortgage interest and others) are 0.9% of returns; the screen also lists refundable credits and credits outside that total, so a little more.
- `abroad_or_territory`: The foreign earned income exclusion (0.27% of returns) counts only part of US citizens abroad (several hundred thousand returns more claim the foreign tax credit or no exclusion), and territory residents with federal returns add a few hundred thousand.
- `investment_interest`: Investment interest is deducted by well under 1 million returns since 2018 (recalled; Table 2.1 is not archived); margin borrowers who do not itemize also answer yes. The microdata's investment_interest_expense flags 18.6% of filing units, the same units as its mortgage interest, an imputation artefact, and was not used.
- `religious_work`: Clergy number a few hundred thousand and employees of religious organizations perhaps 1 to 2 million (BLS, recalled), about 1% of returns.
- `schedule_a_other`: These items fall mostly on itemizers (9.4% of returns) and on a minority of them; gambling losses go with the 1.4% reporting gambling earnings.
- `fbar_required`: About 1.5 million FBARs a year (FinCEN, recalled), some by entities, against 160.6 million returns.
- `clean_vehicle`: The clean vehicle credit was on 0.31% of returns in 2023 and the refuelling property credit 0.07%. Electric vehicle purchases rose in 2024 and 2025 (the credit ended for vehicles acquired after September 2025), and buyers who transferred the credit to a dealer or leased also answer yes, so about 0.8%.
- `schedule2_other`: The largest counted part (Social Security and Medicare tax on tips not reported, 0.36%) and household employment tax (0.15%), homebuyer credit repayment (0.25%) and uncollected tax (0.06%) sum to about 0.8%.
- `pay_outside_w2`: The largest part; the high end sums the parts and taxable dependent care benefits.
- `additional_medicare_items`: Each listed item is under about 0.4% of returns (tips not reported 0.07%, Form 8919 0.03%, RRTA about 0.13%, church employees and territory wages small).
- `cancelled_debt`: Reported cancellation of debt income is on 0.24% of returns; cancelled debt excluded as insolvency, student loan discharge or qualified principal residence debt also answers yes.
- `form5329_other`: All Form 5329 additional taxes are on 3.2% of returns, mostly the early-distribution tax (Part I, prepared); the other parts are a small fraction.
- `spouse_died`: About 3.1 million US deaths a year (CDC, recalled), roughly a third of them of married people, leave about 1 million surviving spouses, about 0.6% of returns.
- `filer_under16`: Primary taxpayers under 18 are 1.1% of returns (Table 1.6); those under 16 are a part of them, taken as about half.
- `qbi_other`: Publicly traded partnership income and cooperative patronage are held by a small part of the investors and farmers above.
- `repayment`: Repayments of wages, unemployment or benefits under a claim of right appear on no counted line; overpayment recoveries of unemployment benefits are the largest group.
- `agency_home_purchase`: The mortgage interest credit was on 0.05% of returns; holders of bond-financed agency mortgages within the nine-year recapture period (roughly 100,000 loans a year, recalled) add more.
- `blind`: Legal blindness affects roughly 1 million US adults (recalled, CDC/NEI order of magnitude); about 0.4% of returns after joint returns. The microdata's is_blind (3.0% of filing units, any member) measures a broader vision-disability question and was not used.
- `charity_limited`: Gifts of appreciated stock, to private foundations or carried over are concentrated among high-income itemizers, a small part of the 9.4% who itemize.
- `excluded_income`: The foreign earned income exclusion is on 0.27% of returns; the housing exclusion and territory exclusions add a little.
- `adoption`: The adoption credit was on 0.03% of returns in 2023; about 100,000 to 120,000 adoptions a year (recalled), over six years about 0.7 million families, about 0.4%.
- `credit_disallowed`: EIC and CTC disallowances after examination are a few hundred thousand a year (recalled order of magnitude) against 24 million EIC returns.
- `form8938`: Form 8938 thresholds ($50,000 single at year end, higher abroad) are several times the FBAR's; a few hundred thousand filers.
- `alimony`: Received and paid summed; a return rarely has both.
- `other_payments`: Form 2439 undistributed gains, the qualified sick and family leave credits (each under 0.05% in 2023) and the other lines 13 items are rare.
- `puerto_rico`: Puerto Rico residents file Form 1040 mainly to claim the refundable child tax credit; a few hundred thousand returns (recalled order of magnitude).
- `scope_other_line16`: The kiddie tax (Form 8615) alone is 0.14% of returns (dependent returns with it, Table 1.7); Forms 8814 and 4972 add a little.
- `rrta`: Railroad employment is about 200,000 workers (Railroad Retirement Board, recalled), about 0.13% of returns.
- `combat_pay`: Service members in combat zones in 2025 number in the tens of thousands (recalled order of magnitude).
- `foreign_trust`: Foreign trust reporting (Form 3520) is filed by tens of thousands of individuals a year (recalled order of magnitude).
