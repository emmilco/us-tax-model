  (helper) Classification from records: A/B/C short-term (held 1 year or less), D/E/F long-term (held more than 1 year); G–L digital assets.

      Inheritances and gifts:
      - Property you inherited is long-term however long you held it, unless the decedent died in 2010 and the executor elected to file Form 8939 (Revenue Procedure 2011-41 then sets the holding period).
      - If your basis in property you received as a gift is the donor's adjusted basis, your holding period began on the day the donor's did.
      - If your basis in a gift is its fair market value, the holding period starts the day after the gift.

      Counting the holding period:
      - To count the holding period, begin on the day after you acquired the property and include the day you sold it.
      - A block acquired on different dates is entered as separate transactions for its short-term and long-term parts.
      - For securities traded on an established market, the holding period begins the day after the trade date you bought them and ends on the trade date you sold them, not the settlement date.
      - U.S. Treasury notes and bonds bought at auction start the day after the Treasury gives notice of acceptance to successful bidders, and those bought by subscription at a specified yield the day after the subscription is submitted.
      - For shares bought for you by an automatic investment service, the holding period starts the day after the bank's purchase date; full shares are bought first and fractional shares last, and a share bought over more than one purchase date has a split holding period.
      - Real property bought under an unconditional contract is held from the day after you received title or took possession and assumed the burdens and privileges of ownership, whichever came first; for real property you repossessed after selling it, include the time you held it before the original sale and after the repossession, but not the time between.

      Holding periods carried over or starting on a set date:
      - Stock or securities bought in a wash sale include the holding period of those sold.
      - New stock from a nontaxable stock dividend or a spin-off, and nontaxable stock rights, include the holding period of the old stock; stock from a taxable stock dividend begins on the date of distribution, stock acquired by exercising stock rights on the date the right was exercised, and property acquired by exercising an option the day after the option was exercised.
      - Investment property acquired in a nontaxable trade, with a basis figured in whole or in part from the property traded, is held from the day after you acquired the old property.
      - Property you elected to treat as sold and reacquired on January 1, 2001 (January 2, 2001, for readily tradable stock) is treated as acquired on that date.
      - Stock received in a demutualization that qualifies as a tax-free reorganization includes the period you held the equity interest in the mutual company.

      Bad debts, money market funds, short sales, options and contracts:
      - A nonbusiness bad debt is short-term, and so is a gain or loss under the net asset value (NAV) method for a money market fund.
      - A short sale is reported in the year it closes.
      - A gain on closing a short sale is short-term if, on the date of the short sale, you held substantially identical property for 1 year or less, or if you acquired substantially identical property after the short sale but on or before the date you closed it; a loss on a short sale is long-term if, on the date of the short sale, you held substantially identical property for more than 1 year.
      - The premium you receive for writing an option that is not exercised (or repurchased) is a short-term capital gain unless you are in the business of writing options; an option you bought and did not exercise is treated as sold on the date it expired.
      - Any capital gain or loss on the sale, exchange or termination of a contract to sell property is short-term however long you held the contract, and a loss on a securities futures contract to sell is treated as a loss on closing a short sale.
