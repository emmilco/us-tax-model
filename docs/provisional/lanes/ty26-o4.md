## Decisions

- **The `plan_max` question no longer offers the limit as the "don't know" answer.** In 2025 "give $5,000 ... or if you
  do not know of a lower limit" was nearly always right, since $5,000 was the statutory limit. In 2026 the $7,500 applies
  only to a plan the employer amended (i2441 p. 7: "Your employer can tell you whether your dependent care plan was
  amended"; its example substitutes $5,000), so the same fallback would overstate the exclusion for an unamended plan.
  New prompt: "What is the most your (or, on a joint return, your spouse's) dependent care plan lets you set aside
  tax-free for 2026? A plan allows more than $5,000, up to $7,500, only if your employer raised its limit." Help: "Your
  employer can tell you whether it raised the plan's limit for 2026. If you were in more than one plan and they allow
  the same maximum, give that maximum." Who could answer wrongly: a filer in an unamended plan who would have given the
  maximum; the prompt names the condition. The answer only changes the return when box 10 is above $5,000. This is a
  question-wording judgment (interview protocol section 2); **owner review recommended.**
- **Line 8 cites the instructions**, not the form line (change-map note). The table file cites i2441 p. 6.
- **Scope of citation edits.** Only citations whose words change because of these entries are set. Citations that only
  moved pages are left to `citations.py --relocate`. Citations of lines this lane touches are written whole for 2026
  (years included), because an operation's values are not relocated.
- **Register ops live in `f2441.yaml`** (keyed by entry id, as R3 placed its own in a form file).
- `amb.2025.dc_line1e_attribution`'s reason ("excluded benefits of $5,000 leave at most $1,000 of the $6,000 limit") is
  left as is: still true for a $5,000 plan, and the owner's wording.
