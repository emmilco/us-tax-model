# What the newly archived sources say about the design's unverified rules

Scope: the statements that `docs/2026-11-11_interview_redesign_slice_design.md` marks as unverified for lack of an
archived source (I.4; the "as I understand" items of D.2; C4a's note in B.2; decision 6 in H; the C.2 rows that rest
on them). Pages are PDF pages of the archived file, counted as the text layers count them (from 1). Quotes are from the
reading-order text (`sources/2025/<id>.cols.txt`). Nothing here proposes wording.

Archived revisions quoted: i8936, i8839, i8801, i3800 and their forms are the 2025 revisions; Schedule R and its
instructions, Form 5498-SA and the Instructions for Forms 1099-SA and 5498-SA are the 2025 revisions from `irs-prior`
(irs.gov now serves the 2026 ones); Form 8834 is Rev. October 2024 and Form 8912 with its instructions Rev. December
2024 (continuous use); Form 8911, its Schedule A and instructions are Rev. December 2025 ("for tax years beginning in
2025 or later").

## 1. Forms 3800, 8396 and 8839 can leave a credit to carry into the next year (I.4; C7; C.2 rows 2d, 4b, 8b)

**Design's statement: right for all three.**

- Form 3800. i3800 p. 3, "Carryback and Carryforward of Unused Credit": "In general, unused general business credits
  may be carried back 1 year and carried forward 20 years." i3800 p. 11: "The information on Parts IV and VI comes from
  your tax returns and schedules for prior years for carryforwards (and subsequent year, for carrybacks). These include
  Form 3800, Schedules K-1, and your related work papers."
- Form 8396. f8396 p. 2, Part II: "If the amount on line 9 is less than the amount on line 7, you may have an unused
  credit to carry forward to the next 3 tax years or until used, whichever comes first. ... If you have any unused
  credit to carry forward to 2026, keep a copy of this form to figure your credit for 2026." The printed form, p. 1,
  line 4: "Enter any 2022 credit carryforward from line 16 of your 2024 Form 8396" (and lines 5, 6 for 2023, 2024).
- Form 8839. i8839 p. 1: "You may be able to take this credit in 2025 if any of the following statements are true. ...
  4. You have a carryforward of an adoption credit from 2024." i8839 p. 7, Line 18: "you may have an unused
  nonrefundable credit to carry forward to the next 5 years or until used, whichever comes first." The printed form,
  p. 1, line 15: "Credit carryforward, if any, from prior years. See your Adoption Credit Carryforward Worksheet in the
  2024 Form 8839 instructions".

## 2. Forms 8859 and 8834 are carryforward-only (I.4, "confirmed by i1040gi p. 116"; C7; C.2 rows 9, 10)

**Form 8859: right.** f8859 p. 1, Purpose of Form: "Use Form 8859 to claim a carryforward of the District of Columbia
first-time homebuyer credit from 2024." and "Caution: You may claim only a carryforward of any unused credit (which is
shown on your prior year Form 8859, line 4)." Line 1: "Credit carryforward from 2024. Enter the amount from line 4 of
your 2024 Form 8859".

**Form 8834: right that only an earlier year's credit is claimed; not right that the 2024 Form 8834 is where it shows.**
f8834 p. 1, Notes: "Use this form to claim qualified electric vehicle passive activity credits from prior years
(allowed on Form 8582-CR or Form 8810 for the current year). The qualified electric vehicle credit was available for
certain vehicles placed in service before 2007." f8834 p. 2, Line 1: "Enter the qualified electric vehicle passive
activity credits allowed for your current tax year from Form 8582-CR, Passive Activity Credit Limitations". f8834 p. 2,
Line 7: "If you can't use part of the credit because of the tax liability limit, the unused credit is lost. The unused
or excess credit can't be carried back or forward to other tax years." So Form 8834 itself carries nothing to the next
year; the amount waiting from earlier years is an unallowed passive activity credit, kept on Form 8582-CR (not
archived). A filer can have such a credit for 2025 without a Form 8834 in the 2024 return. (One more route: i8801
p. 4, Line 20, an "electric vehicle credit not allowed for 2024 solely because of the limitation under section
30(b)(3)(B)" goes into the Form 8801 credit.)

## 3. A dealer who took the vehicle credit off the price still obliges the buyer to report it (I.4; C1a's help)

**Design's statement: right.** i8936 p. 1, Purpose of Form: "Caution: You must file Form 8936 and Schedule A (Form
8936) if you purchased a new or used clean vehicle from a registered dealer and reduced the amount you paid at the time
of sale by transferring the credit to the dealer." Also f8936 p. 1 and f8936sa p. 1, Notes: "Individuals who
transferred the credit to the dealer at the time of sale must file this form and Schedule A (Form 8936)." And a
repayment can follow: i8936 p. 2, "Repayment of credit. If you purchased a new clean vehicle from a registered dealer
and reduced the amount you paid at the time of sale by transferring the credit to the dealer and you no longer qualify
for the credit when you file your tax return, you must repay the amount of the credit that you transferred to the
dealer." (the same for a previously owned vehicle, p. 4).

## 4. A lessee has no credit of their own (D.2, C1 item 1)

**Design's statement: right.** i8936 p. 2 (new clean vehicle), p. 3 (previously owned) and p. 5 (commercial): "You are
the owner of the vehicle. If the vehicle is leased, only the lessor and not the lessee is entitled to the credit."
f8936sa p. 2, Part II (and the matching questions of Part IV, p. 2, and Part V, p. 3): "Did you acquire the vehicle for
use or to lease to others, and not for resale? Answer "No" if you are leasing the vehicle from another person."

## 5. Whether the purchase date or the delivery date counts (D.2, C1 item 2)

**Design's suspicion ("the credit may turn on the delivery date instead"): right, and there is a second date.**

- The year of the credit is the year the vehicle is placed in service. i8936 p. 1: "Use Form 8936 and Schedule A (Form
  8936) to figure the following credits for clean vehicles you placed in service during your tax year." i8936 p. 2: "You
  placed the vehicle in service during the tax year." i8936 p. 7, Line 3: "The date the vehicle was placed in service is
  the date the taxpayer takes possession of the vehicle." So a vehicle bought in 2024 and received in 2025 belongs to
  the 2025 return, and one received in 2026 does not.
- For 2025 the acquisition date also matters. i8936 p. 1, What's New: "Clean vehicles acquired after September 30, 2025.
  Taxpayers cannot claim clean vehicle credits for new, previously owned, or commercial clean vehicles that they
  acquired after September 30, 2025. ... a vehicle is "acquired" as of the date a written binding contract is entered
  into and a payment has been made. A payment includes a nominal down payment or a vehicle trade-in." f8936sa p. 1,
  line 5: "Does the VIN entered on line 2 belong to a new clean vehicle acquired before October 1, 2025 and placed in
  service during the tax year?"

## 6. Adoption costs paid in a year before the adoption is final are credited the year after (D.2, C2 item 2)

**Design's statement: right for a U.S. child; different for a foreign child.**

- i8839 p. 3, table "Domestic Adoption": "IF you pay qualifying expenses in... any year before the year the adoption
  becomes final* / THEN take the credit in... the year after the year of the payment."; "the year the adoption becomes
  final / the year the adoption becomes final."; "any year after the year the adoption becomes final / the year of the
  payment." Its note: "*In the case of a U.S. child, an adoption credit may be allowable for an attempted or
  unsuccessful adoption, as well as for an adoption that isn't final." i8839 p. 3: "If the eligible child is a U.S.
  citizen or resident, you can take the adoption credit or exclusion even if the adoption never became final."
- i8839 p. 1 gives the 2025 cases: "1. You paid qualified adoption expenses in connection with the adoption of an
  eligible U.S. child ... in: a. 2024 and the adoption wasn't final at the end of 2024, or b. 2025 and the adoption
  became final in or before 2025. 2. You adopted an eligible U.S. child with special needs and the adoption became final
  in 2025. (In this case, you may be able to take the credit even if you didn't pay any qualified adoption expenses.)
  3. You paid qualified adoption expenses in connection with the adoption of an eligible foreign child in:
  a. 2025 or prior years and the adoption became final in 2025, or b. 2025 and the adoption became final before 2025. See Column (e), later.
  4. You have a carryforward of an adoption credit from 2024."
- Foreign child, i8839 p. 3: "If the eligible child is a foreign child, you can't take the adoption credit or exclusion
  unless the adoption becomes final." Table "Foreign Adoption": expenses paid in "any year before the year the adoption
  becomes final" are taken in "the year the adoption becomes final."
- So the residual the design describes is real: a person who paid in 2024 toward a U.S. adoption not final at the end
  of 2024, and who neither adopted nor paid in 2025, has a 2025 credit (case 1a) and no 2024 Form 8839. Conversely, a
  person who paid in 2025 toward a U.S. adoption not final in 2025 has no 2025 credit for those costs (they are taken
  in 2026).

## 7. The credit does not apply to the adoption of a spouse's child (D.2, C2 item 3)

**Design's statement: right.** i8839 p. 2: "Qualified adoption expenses don't include expenses: ... • For the adoption
of your spouse's child;".

## 8. Schedule R: who is asked, the disability income, and "only for people with low income" (D.2, C4 item 1; B.2 C4a; C.2 row 6; decision 5)

**Design's statements: right.**

- Who qualifies (the basis of asking C4a of a person 65 or older, or retired on disability). i1040sr p. 1, Qualified
  Individual: "You are a qualified individual for this credit if you are a U.S. citizen or resident alien (or you elect
  to be treated as a resident alien) and either of the following applies. 1. You were age 65 or older at the end of
  2025. 2. You were under age 65 (discussed later) at the end of 2025 and all three of the following statements are
  true. a. You retired on permanent and total disability. ... b. You receive taxable disability income for 2025. c. On
  January 1, 2025, you had not reached mandatory retirement age".
- Disability income must come from an employer's plan. i1040sr p. 2, Disability Income: "If you are under age 65, you
  must also have taxable disability income to qualify for the credit. Disability income must meet both of the following
  requirements. 1. It must be paid under your employer's accident or health plan or pension plan. 2. It must be included
  in your income as wages (or payments instead of wages) for the time you are absent from work because of permanent and
  total disability."
- Low income. i1040sr p. 1: "You can take the credit ... if you meet both of the following requirements. • You are a
  qualified individual. • Your income isn't more than certain limits." i1040sr p. 3, Income Limits: "If your AGI or your
  nontaxable pensions, annuities, or disability income are equal to or more than the income limits, you can't take the
  credit." i1040sr p. 6, Table 1 (the amount on Form 1040 or 1040-SR line 11a; then the total of nontaxable social
  security and other nontaxable pensions, annuities or disability income): single, head of household or qualifying
  surviving spouse, "$17,500 or more", "$5,000"; married filing jointly and only one spouse a qualifying individual,
  "$20,000 or more", "$5,000"; married filing jointly and both, "$25,000 or more", "$7,500"; married filing separately
  and lived apart all of 2025, "$12,500 or more", "$3,750". (Table 1 was read from the layout text, `i1040sr.txt`, where
  its rows stay together.)

## 9. Form 8801's filing rule (D.2, C7 items 2 and 3: "no archived source describes the form's filing rule")

**Now described; the design's residual is real and somewhat wider than stated.** i8801 p. 1, Who Should File: "Complete
Form 8801 if you are an individual, estate, or trust that for 2024 had: • An AMT liability and adjustments or
preferences other than exclusion items, • A credit carryforward to 2025 (on 2024 Form 8801, line 26), or • An unallowed
qualified electric vehicle credit (see the instructions for line 20). File Form 8801 only if line 21 is more than
zero." i8801 p. 4, Line 21: "If line 21 is zero or less, you don't have a minimum tax credit or a credit carryforward.
Don't complete the rest of this form and don't file it." The printed form, p. 2, line 19: "2024 credit carryforward.
Enter the amount from your 2024 Form 8801, line 26".

What follows for C7's test ("does your 2024 return include Form 8801"): a carryforward from 2023 or earlier shows on a
2024 Form 8801 (line 26) whenever one was filed, which is the case C7 catches. A person whose first alternative minimum
tax was for 2024 has a 2024 return with Form 6251 and no Form 8801, and is told by the first bullet to complete Form
8801 for 2025; C7 does not reach that person.

## 10. Form 5498-SA exists and has a box for "Archer MSA" (I.4; the help of H0 and H3)

**Design's statement: right.** f5498sa p. 2 (Copy B, For Participant), box 6: "6 HSA / Archer MSA / MA MSA" (three
checkboxes). f5498sa p. 3, Instructions for Participant: "Box 6. Shows the type of account that is reported on this
Form 5498-SA." Also p. 3: "Note: Don't attach Form 5498-SA to your income tax return. Instead, keep it for your
records." The trustee's side, i1099sa p. 4, "Box 6. Checkbox". The archived Form 1099-SA instructions for the trustee
are in the same document (i1099sa pp. 1 to 3).

## Noticed while reading, outside the marked rules (not acted on)

- C9 asks whether the filer holds a Form 1097-BTC. i8912 p. 3, Part IV: "Complete and attach a Part IV for each bond
  you held or bond credits received from a pass-through entity that weren't reported to you on Form(s) 1097-BTC." A
  holder of a tax credit bond can have the credit without that paper. i8912 p. 3 also allows a carryover: "you may carry
  the unused portion of the credit to the next tax year" (a qualified tax credit bond or a build America bond); Form
  8912 is not among C7's six numbers.
- C.2 row 11 (charger). i8911 p. 1: the personal part is for property "placed in service at your main home"; the
  business part "is treated as a general business credit". i8911 p. 3: "The unused personal portion of the credit can't
  be carried back or forward to other tax years." i8911 p. 1, What's New: the credit's termination date is now June 30,
  2026.
- C3's refusal text says the credit changes the mortgage interest deduction. f8396 p. 2, "Reduction of home mortgage
  interest deduction on Schedule A (Form 1040)": "If you itemize your
  deductions on Schedule A, you must reduce the amount of home mortgage interest you would otherwise deduct on Schedule
  A by the amount on Form 8396, line 3". D.2 C3 item 2 (a reissued certificate after refinancing): f8396 p. 2,
  Refinanced Mortgage, "You can refinance your mortgage without losing this credit if your existing MCC is reissued and
  the reissued certificate meets all of the following conditions."
- Schedule A (Form 3800), which the Form 3800 instructions name as a transfer election statement, was not archived: the
  address tried on www.irs.gov did not download. Schedule A (Form 8911) was archived with Form 8911.
