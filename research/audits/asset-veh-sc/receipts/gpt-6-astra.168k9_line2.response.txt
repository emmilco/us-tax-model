**2. My reading is no for all three, subject to the qualifications below.**

**(a) Land improvement: No**, assuming an ordinary nonqualifying land improvement, such as general paving or landscaping. It fails §179(d)(1)’s qualifying-property definition, ordinarily because it is neither §1245 property nor elected qualified real property. Classification matters: something colloquially called a land improvement could qualify if it actually satisfies those statutory categories. [§179(d)(1)](https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/119-121/t26/s179/)

**(b) Disqualified noncorporate-lessor property: No.** Section 179(d)(5) says **“This section shall not apply”** to that property unless an exception applies. Although it calls the asset “section 179 property,” its exclusion reaches the entire section, including §179(b)(2), not merely the deduction election. This is my statutory reading; the line-2 instructions do not expressly resolve this particular distinction. [§179(d)(5)](https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/119-121/t26/s179/)

**(c) Government-used property: No**, unless an applicable exception—particularly a lease shorter than six months—applies. Thus, absent an exception, it is excluded from the definition itself and from line 2 and the phaseout calculation. [§50(b)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/50)
