# 2025 additional standard deduction — soft corroboration

- Record: secondary.2025.standard_extra; researched 2026-10-24.
- Affected lines: sa.extra, sa.extra_age65, sa.extra_blind, sa.standardraw.
- Reading: a dollar increment per qualifying age/blindness box, added to the base deduction.
  Competing reading raised by the packet: multiplying the age count by the blindness count.
- Primary anchor: archived i1040gi PDF p.35 chart, with base amounts on f1040 PDF p.2.
  For Single, $17750−$15750=$2000; the second box adds another $2000. For MFJ,
  $33100−$31500=$1600 and successive chart rows increase by $1600.
- Secondary publisher/author: Tax Foundation / Alex Durante, “2025 Tax Brackets”.
- URL: https://taxfoundation.org/data/all/federal/2025-tax-brackets/
- Retrieved: 2026-10-24; opened article, Standard Deduction section.
- Observed date display: January 1, 2026 and September 1, 2026; these are page metadata,
  not asserted effective dates of the tax rules. The article explicitly discusses tax year 2025.
- Short verbatim excerpt: “$2,000 for single filers and $1,600 for joint filers (per eligible spouse)”.
  The accompanying `.excerpt.txt` and `.sha256` preserve only this passage, not the whole page.
- Primary references/lineage: the article's deduction table cites IRS Publication17 (2025).
  It is an outside explanation of IRS material, not independent calculation evidence.
- Scope of support: the two age-related increment amounts for Single and joint filers.
  It does not establish blindness, the HOH/MFS/QSS branches, age-at-death, the exact birth-date
  cutoff, or every eligibility condition. Its loose “over age65” language must not define the cutoff.
- Contrary material: none found in this limited example. No exhaustive consensus search is claimed.
  The owner's objection is about the packet's source alignment and confusing units; that remains valid.
- Assessment: corroborates the two amounts. The archived chart supports their per-box application.
  The code multiplies dollar amounts by counts separately, then adds; it does not multiply the
  age count by the blindness count. Improve the presentation; do not change the saved verdicts or
  count a new worked-value/H assertion from this research.

Current IRS Topic551 also explicitly explains the age/blindness additions, including their sum:
https://www.irs.gov/taxtopics/tc551 (opened 2026-10-24). This is additional IRS explanatory material,
not secondary commentary, and is not silently added to the archived authoritative source manifest.
