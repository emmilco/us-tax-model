# Ten 2025 returns with education expenses, to work by hand from the IRS text

Each return is for tax year 2025, on Form 1040, with Form 8863. Unless a return says otherwise:

- Filers are 40 years old, not blind, U.S. citizens with valid SSNs (valid for employment), who lived all year in one
  of the 48 contiguous states. Nobody can claim them as dependents, and nobody does. Nobody married, divorced or
  separated in 2025. Head of household returns qualify for that status.
- Income is wages only (Form W-2 box 1), with no other income, adjustments, tax-exempt interest or excluded foreign or
  Puerto Rico income. The standard deduction is taken. There are no other credits.
- Children listed are qualifying children claimed as dependents. They are U.S. citizens with SSNs valid for employment,
  lived with the filer all year (time away at school is a temporary absence), did not provide over half of their own
  support, and are not required to file a return. A child described as a student is a full-time student.
- Every student named below is a student at an eligible educational institution. Each received a 2025 Form 1098-T from
  that institution, and the institution's employer identification number (EIN) is on it, unless the return says
  otherwise. Amounts given were actually paid in 2025, by the filer, for academic periods beginning in 2025, and none
  was paid with a Coverdell, qualified tuition program (529) or other tax-free distribution. "Required tuition and
  fees" are amounts required to be paid to the institution as a condition of enrollment. "Books not required" are
  books and supplies needed for the course of study but bought elsewhere and not required to be paid to the
  institution.
- Unless stated: no student received tax-free educational assistance or a refund of 2025 expenses; no American
  opportunity credit (or Hope credit) was claimed for the student in any earlier year; the student was enrolled at
  least half-time in a program leading to a degree for at least one academic period beginning in 2025; the student
  had not completed the first four years of postsecondary education before 2025; nobody was convicted of a felony drug
  offense; no education credit was ever denied or reduced, so Form 8862 is not needed. In any academic period the
  tax-free assistance and refunds stated are less than that period's expenses.
- Ages are at the end of 2025. Where a filer's birth date is given, use it.

## The returns

- **E1.** Single. Wages $84,567. The filer is the student: a first-year undergraduate enrolled half-time in a degree
  program. Required tuition and fees $3,100; books not required $450.
- **E2.** Married filing jointly with one child, a son aged 20 who is a full-time sophomore. Wages $98,000 (the
  taxpayer) and $72,866 (the spouse). The son: required tuition and fees $4,800; books not required $350; a Pell Grant
  of $1,000, tax-free and applied to his 2025 tuition. The spouse is also a student: she completed her bachelor's
  degree before 2025 and is enrolled half-time in a master's program; required tuition and fees $9,000.
- **E3.** Head of household with one child, a daughter aged 22, a full-time student. Wages $62,000. The daughter
  completed her bachelor's degree in 2024 and is now in a full-time graduate program: required tuition and fees $7,200.
  The filer took two professional courses at a university to improve her job skills, not as part of any degree program
  (she has not completed four years of postsecondary education): required tuition and fees $5,000.
- **E4.** Married filing jointly with two children: a son aged 18, a full-time first-year university student, and a
  daughter aged 7. Wages $30,000 (the taxpayer) and $15,000 (the spouse). The son: required tuition and fees $4,000.
- **E5.** Single, born May 14, 2008. Wages $30,000, which paid for more than half of her support; she lives on her own
  and her parents (both alive) cannot claim her. She is the student: a full-time first-year undergraduate (early
  admission). Required tuition and fees $4,000.
- **E6.** Single, born March 10, 2007. Wages $22,000. Her total support for 2025 was $50,000, of which $28,000 came from
  her own savings (an inheritance she received in 2022, which produced no 2025 income); she provided all of her own
  support and nobody can claim her. Both parents are alive. She is the student: full-time (enrolled January to May and
  September to December), first-year. Required tuition and fees $3,000; books not required $400.
- **E7.** Single, born August 22, 2003. Wages $26,000. Her total support for 2025 was $60,000, the rest from her own
  savings (an inheritance received in 2021, which produced no 2025 income); nobody can claim her. Both parents are
  alive. She is the student: a full-time junior (enrolled January to May and September to December) in a degree
  program. Required tuition and fees $5,200. She received a $1,500 scholarship, which under its terms could be used
  only for tuition and which she excludes from income.
- **E8.** Single, born February 11, 2004. Wages $35,000. His total support for 2025 was $80,000, the rest from his own
  savings (an inheritance received in 2019, which produced no 2025 income); nobody can claim him. Both of his parents
  died in 2019. He is the student: a full-time sophomore (enrolled January to May and September to December).
  Required tuition and fees $4,000; books not required $600.
- **E9.** Head of household with two children: a son aged 20, a full-time student, and a daughter aged 12. Wages
  $58,000. The son began college courses at 16; the American opportunity credit was claimed for him for 2021, 2022,
  2023 and 2024, and he has not completed the first four years of postsecondary education. His required tuition and
  fees $8,000; books not required $700. The filer is also a student: a first-year student enrolled half-time in an
  associate degree program at a community college; required tuition and fees $1,800. She received the college's 2025
  Form 1098-T, but the college's EIN is not on it and she could not obtain it before filing.
- **E10.** Single, 30 years old. Wages $86,782. The filer is the student: she took one course in a degree program,
  enrolled less than half-time (she has not completed four years of postsecondary education). Required tuition and
  fees $6,000; books not required $300. After she dropped a lab section in 2025, the university refunded $400 of her
  2025 tuition to her in 2025.

## What to report

For each return, in dollars and cents (decimals as decimals): Form 8863 lines 1 to 19, including the line 6 and
line 17 decimals and whether the line 7 box is checked; for each student, Part III lines 23 to 26 ("yes", "no" or
"not reached") and lines 27 to 31; Credit Limit Worksheet lines 1 to 7; Schedule 3 line 3; Form 1040 lines 18, 19,
22, 24 and 29. Use "skipped" or "blank" where the form or instructions say to skip a line or leave it blank, and say
why. For every value, a short derivation naming the line, worksheet or table and page. Where the text is ambiguous,
say so and give the reading you used.
