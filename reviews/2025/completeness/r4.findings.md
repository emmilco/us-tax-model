## PENDING-r4-1. Home costs paid from a qualifying person's own funds are not attributed in Test 2 (household#1). Proposed severity: Medium

- **Source.** p501 p. 9 (Keeping Up a Home): "Amounts paid out of funds received in the child's or qualifying person's
  name, such as Social Security, are considered paid by the child, not you."
- **Current encoding.** BUG-0258 added the cost definition to both `dependent.parent_home_paid` and
  `household.home_cost`, but the own-funds rule only to `dependent.parent_home_paid` ("Money your parent spent on the
  home out of their own income, such as Social Security, counts as paid by your parent, not by you."). `household.home_cost`
  asks "Did you pay over half the cost of keeping up your home for 2025?" with the included and excluded costs only.
  Two errors are plausible:
  - a parent who receives a child's survivor or SSI benefits as payee pays the rent from them and counts it as their own;
  - a filer whose live-in parent or other qualifying person puts their Social Security toward the household bills counts
    those bills as paid by the filer.
- **Witness.** Base `stage8.hoh.child.ctc`, on the current tree:
  - `household.home_cost` Yes gives head of household, line 16 $14,378 and line 24 $12,153.
  - No refuses head of household. Filed single, line 16 is $17,867 and line 24 $15,642.
- **Why Medium.** It is the same sentence as PENDING-r3-1 (Medium), on the Test 2 question, which is the commoner head of
  household case. A child receiving Social Security survivor benefits and a parent on Social Security living with the
  filer are common qualifying persons.
- **Minimal fix.** Append to `household.home_cost`: "Amounts paid out of money received in the name of your child or
  other qualifying person, such as their Social Security, count as paid by that person, not by you."
- **Sibling, not claimed.** `household.qss_eligible` test 4 ("You paid over half the cost of keeping up your home") still
  carries neither the cost definition nor this rule; the same sentences fit it.
- **Also noted.** `dependent.parent_home_paid` says "money your parent spent … out of their own income"; the source's
  "funds received in the qualifying person's name" also covers the parent's money spent by the filer as payee. "Amounts
  paid out of your parent's own income" would close that reading.
- **The rest of the claim.** TANF you receive and use for another person is support you provide (p501 p. 9), which the
  natural answer already counts as yours. Keeping up the home during a temporary absence is within "for 2025".

## Low (for T-092)

- **L1. The support question omits the excluded items and the insurance rules (dependent#1).** Repeat of round-3 L1,
  wider.
  - **Not in `dependent.supported`.** Medical insurance premiums you pay are your support, and Medicare and other
    insurance benefits are not (p501 p. 21). Income and social security taxes the person pays, life insurance premiums
    and funeral expenses are not support (p501 p. 21).
  - **Why low.** Counting a parent's life insurance premiums or funeral costs could carry a filer over half; support
    definitions were rated Low in round-1 PENDING-8 and round-3 L1.
  - **Covered or rare.** Covered in substance: lodging, own funds actually spent, the allotment, care you pay for.
    Arithmetic: household-expense division, fair market value, capital items. Rare: fiscal year, borrowed money repaid
    later, lump-sum home-for-the-aged payment, foster expenses of a business or charitable caregiver, one check to both
    spouses, GI Bill.
- **L2. `dependent.self_support` omits GI Bill, DEA, insurance benefits and taxes (dependent#2).** Repeat of round-3 L2,
  with Medicare benefits and the person's own income and social security taxes added (p501 p. 21). The tax rule needs a
  working child whose withheld taxes would carry them past half their own support. Candidate wording: "Do not count
  income or social security taxes the person paid, or Medicare or other insurance benefits."
- **L3. A revoked Form 8332 release is not timed (dependent#7).** Repeat of round-3 L3; unchanged.
- **L4. The American opportunity credit accepts an applied-for TIN (dependent#10).** New.
  - **The rule.** The credit is not allowed for a student who has not been issued a TIN by the due date (i8863 pp. 1
    and 3).
  - **The encoding.** `dependent.edu_student` reads `dependent.tin`, which also answers Yes for an ITIN or ATIN only
    applied for by then. `amb.2025.ctc_spouse_itin_application` confines the deeming of an applied-for ITIN to the
    credit for other dependents.
  - **Witness.** Base `stage19.example.jack_kay`: `dependent[k0].tin` Yes gives line 29 $1,000 and Form 8863 line 19
    $1,500; No refuses the return (`dependent.claimed_no_tin`). For a student whose ITIN is pending, Yes overstates the
    credit and No refuses, so no answer is right.
  - **Why low.** It needs a dependent student who needs an ITIN and whose application is pending at the due date; the
    rarity of round-1 PENDING-10 (expired ITIN, Low).
  - **Candidate fix.** A separate input for an issued TIN read only by `dependent.edu_student`, or a refusal when the TIN
    was only applied for and the education credits are claimed.
