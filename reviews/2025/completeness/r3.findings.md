## PENDING-r3-1. The cost of keeping up a parent's home is asked without its definition (dependent#7). Proposed severity: Medium

- **Source.** p501 p. 9 (Keeping Up a Home):
  - "Include in the cost of keeping up a home expenses such as rent, mortgage interest, real estate taxes, insurance on
    the home, repairs, utilities, and food eaten in the home."
  - "Don't include the cost of clothing, education, medical treatment, vacations, life insurance, or transportation.
    Also don't include the value of your services or those of a member of your household."
  - "Amounts paid out of funds received in the child's or qualifying person's name, such as Social Security, are
    considered paid by the child, not you."
- **Current encoding.** `dependent.parent_home_paid` asks: "did you pay over half the cost of keeping up a home that
  was their main home for all of 2025 …?" It does not say which costs count, or whose money the parent's own benefits
  are. Two errors are plausible:
  - a filer who pays a parent's medical bills, car or clothing counts them;
  - a filer who manages a parent's Social Security pays the rent from it and counts the payment as their own.
- **Witness.** Base `stage8.hoh.parent.test1`, rerun on the current tree:
  - `parent_home_paid` Yes gives head of household, with line 16 at $14,378 and line 24 at $13,878.
  - No gives the refusal `household.hoh_fails`. Filed single, line 24 is $17,367.
- **Why Medium.** Test 1 with a parent who lives apart is a common head of household case. A parent on Social Security
  is the commonest qualifying relative. Round-1 PENDING-6, on the same question and with the same witness, was rated
  Medium.
- **Minimal fix.** Append to the question: "The cost of keeping up a home includes rent, mortgage interest, real estate
  taxes, insurance on the home, repairs, utilities and food eaten in the home. It does not include clothing, education,
  medical treatment, vacations, life insurance, transportation, or the value of anyone's services. Amounts paid from
  your parent's own funds, such as their Social Security, are paid by your parent, not you."
- **Sibling, not claimed.** `household.home_cost` (Test 2) and the cost-of-home test of `household.qss_eligible` (test
  4) ask the same thing with no definition. The household review did not claim them. The same definition fits both, and
  a fix would naturally carry it to all three.

## Low (for T-092)

- **L1. Medical insurance and Medicare are not addressed in the support question (dependent#3).**
  - **The rule.** Medical insurance premiums you pay are support you provide. Medical insurance benefits, including
    Medicare, are not support (p501 p. 21).
  - **What `dependent.supported` says.** Neither.
  - **Why low.** The natural answer leaves Medicare out, and that answer is correct. The wrong answer needs a filer to
    count Medicare as the government's support.
  - **The rest of the claim.** It is covered in substance or rare. Covered: lodging at fair rental value, own funds
    actually spent, and allotments. Arithmetic: the over-half division and fair market value. Rare: fiscal year, the
    lump-sum home-for-the-aged payment, foster-care expenses, a joint check, needs-based payments and the GI Bill.
  - **Precedent.** Round-1 PENDING-8 rated support-definition omissions Low.
- **L2. `dependent.self_support` omits three rules (dependent#4).**
  - **State benefits.** State benefits to a needy person are the state's support, not the person's (p501 p. 20).
    BUG-0252 put this rule in `supported` only.
  - **GI Bill.** GI Bill amounts are in total support.
  - **DEA payments.** Survivors' and Dependents' Educational Assistance used for the child's support is excluded.
  - **Why low.** The state-benefit case needs a child who receives needs-based benefits in their own name, for example
    SSI for a disabled child, and those benefits must exceed half of the child's total support. GI Bill and DEA students
    are rare. Candidate wording: "Benefits a state provides to the person as a needy person are support provided by the
    state, not by the person."
- **L3. A revoked Form 8332 release is not timed (dependent#8).**
  - **The rule.** A custodial parent's revocation is effective for 2025 only if written notice was given in 2024 or
    earlier (p501 pp. 14-15).
  - **The error.** `dependent.divorced_rule` does not say this. A custodial parent who revoked in 2025 plausibly answers
    No and claims the child. The release still holds for 2025, so the answer should be Yes, which refuses.
  - **Why low.** It needs a release revoked in 2025 specifically.
  - **Candidate wording.** "A release you revoked counts as still in effect for 2025 unless you gave the other parent
    written notice of the revocation in 2024 or earlier."
