## Notes on the claims

- **household#3 (introduced).**
  - **Source.** i1040gi p. 15: "Your home was the main home of your child, stepchild, or foster child for more than half
    of 2025 (if half or less, see Exception to time lived with you, earlier)". p501 p. 8, test 4, points to the birth,
    death and temporary-absence rules.
  - **Before.** The question stated no rule, so it did not lead a filer to a literal No.
  - **Now.** A married filer living apart whose child was born late in 2025 answers No and is refused (W2), where Yes
    computes head of household (W1).
  - **Same finding elsewhere.** The Claude render makes the same objection and a second one ("claims" for "can claim",
    findings F2), which this claim does not raise.
- **household#4 (predates).**
  - **What the claim cites.** p501 p. 8, "If you were considered married for part of the year". That sentence sits under
    Considered Unmarried and reads most naturally of a married person considered unmarried, whom
    `hoh_community_property` refuses.
  - **What reaches the divorced filer.** i1040gi p. 23: "If you and your spouse lived in a community property state, you
    must usually follow state law".
  - **The gap.** No gate reaches a filer no longer married at the end of 2025 (W5 computes).
  - **Severity.** Whether any income is community turns on state law, which is not archived. The owner's decision 10
    covered only the married head of household.
- **household#5.** `scope.other_income` asks "Any nonresident alien status (you or your spouse, for any part of 2025)"
  and refuses. So neither the election statement nor the name entry of i1040gi p. 14 is ever reached. The same holds for
  condition 3 of the new `household.unmarried`.
- **household#1 and #2.** These are again rules asked or refused on `dependent`, which the household prompt does not
  show (r6 and r7, "What this suggests").
