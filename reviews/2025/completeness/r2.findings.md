## PENDING-r2-1. The support questions still omit the scholarship and state-benefit rules (dependent#5, #6). Proposed severity: Medium

This extends BUG-0223, whose fix carried five Pub. 501 rules but not these.

- **Source.**
  - p501 p. 15: "A scholarship received by a child who is a student isn't taken into account in determining whether the
    child provided more than half of their own support." p501 p. 21 also excludes "Scholarships received by your child if
    your child is a student" from total support.
  - p501 p. 20: "Benefits provided by the state to a needy person are generally considered support provided by the
    state." The heading lists welfare, food benefits and housing.
  - p501 p. 20: "If a child receives social security benefits and uses them toward their own support, the benefits are
    considered as provided by the child."
- **Current encoding.**
  - `dependent.self_support` counts the person's own funds when they are actually spent. A scholarship spent on tuition
    and housing reads as the student's own funds.
  - `dependent.supported` mentions only public assistance that the filer received. Nothing says that state benefits paid
    to the person are the state's support.
- **Witnesses.**
  - **Scholarship.** Base `stage19.example.jack_kay`, with the student's gross income set to $8,000 so that the
    qualifying-relative fallback does not apply. With `self_support` No (the source answer for a student whose
    scholarship pays over half), line 19 is $500, line 29 is $1,000 and Schedule 3 line 3 is $1,500. With Yes, all three
    are $0. With gross income under $5,200, the qualifying-relative path absorbs the error (run with $0: no change).
  - **State benefits.** Base `stage8.hoh.parent.test1`. With `supported` Yes, the return is head of household, with line
    16 at $14,378 and line 19 at $500. With No, the source answer when state benefits exceed the filer's share, the
    result is the refusal `household.hoh_fails`. Filed single, it is $500 against $0.
- **Why Medium.** A college student whose scholarship covers most of their costs is common, and the dependent's
  American opportunity credit goes with the dependency.
- **Minimal fix.**
  - Add to `self_support`: "Do not count a scholarship the person received as a student. Social security benefits the
    person receives and uses for their own support are support the person provided."
  - Add to `supported`: "Benefits a state provides to the person as a needy person (such as welfare, food benefits or
    housing) are support provided by the state, not by you. Do not count a scholarship your child received as a
    student."
- **Not proposed.** The rest of dependent#6 is covered in substance or is rare: the BAQ is lodging the filer provides;
  the total-support items and household-expense division are arithmetic of the over-half test; the fiscal-year,
  lump-sum home-for-the-aged, joint-check and unreimbursed-foster-expense rules are rare.

## PENDING-r2-2. Gross income is asked without its definition (dependent#8). Proposed severity: Medium

- **Source.** p501 p. 19, Gross income defined: "Gross income is all income in the form of money, property, and services
  that isn't exempt from tax. … Gross receipts from rental property are gross income. Don't deduct taxes, repairs, or
  other expenses … Gross income includes a partner's share of the gross (not net) partnership income. Gross income also
  includes all taxable unemployment compensation, taxable social security benefits, and certain amounts received as
  scholarship and fellowship grants."
- **Current encoding.** `dependent.gross_income` asks only "This person's gross income for 2025". Two errors are
  plausible:
  - entering net rental or partnership income;
  - entering total, not taxable, social security benefits. This is common for a dependent parent.
- **Witness.** Base `stage8.hoh.parent.test1` (hoh). A parent has $9,000 of gross rents and $5,000 of expenses.
  - Entered as the net $4,000: head of household, with line 16 at $14,378 and line 19 at $500.
  - Any amount of $5,200 or more, as the source requires (run with $6,000): the refusal `household.hoh_fails`.
  - Filed single: $500 against $0.

  The error in the other direction also changes the result. A parent with $3,000 of interest and $15,000 of
  nontaxable benefits who is entered as $18,000 is denied the $500 credit for other dependents.
- **Why Medium.** The qualifying relative is a parent on social security in the commonest case.
- **Minimal fix.** Word the question as: "This person's gross income for 2025: all income in the form of money, property
  and services that isn't exempt from tax. Include gross rents without deducting expenses, a partner's share of gross
  (not net) partnership income, business sales less cost of goods sold, and taxable unemployment compensation, taxable
  social security benefits and taxable scholarships. Do not include nontaxable social security benefits or other
  tax-exempt income."
