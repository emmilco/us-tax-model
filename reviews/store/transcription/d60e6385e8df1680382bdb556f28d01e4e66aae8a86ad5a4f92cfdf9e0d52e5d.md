Second transcription's note: The IRS text defines this as filing status married filing separately and living with the spouse at any time during the year. The return's filing status is not in the supplied interface, so it is detected through the filing-status-keyed parameter f8582.special_max, which is $12,500 only for mfs. The interface also has no answer to whether the filer lived with the spouse. This encoding takes the conservative reading and treats every mfs filer as having lived with the spouse. As a result, an mfs filer who lived apart all year is denied the Part IV/Part II treatment. A living-arrangement line is needed to make this exact.
