(a) — Kind of property
Status: **MATCH**

Model: Residential tracks "a building in which 80% or more of the total rent is from dwelling units". Nonresidential tracks "any real property that is neither residential rental property nor property with a class life of less than 27.5 years". The personal category (appliances, carpets, furniture, fences) is outside 27.5/39-year real property and is refused.

(b) (rprop.month) — Month placed in service
Status: **MATCH**

Model: The text says "enter the month and year you placed the property in service" and, for converted personal-use property, to use "the conversion date". The ready-and-available wording is a reasonable restatement of placed in service.

<!-- part section (b) (rprop.year) -->
(b) (rprop.year) — Year placed in service
Status: **MATCH**

Model: The year placed in service matches column (b). Pre-1987 property is not MACRS ("MACRS is used to depreciate any tangible property placed in service after 1986"). Collapsing those years into before1987 only feeds the recovery-year lookup, and pre-1994 nonresidential property is refused.

<!-- part section (c) -->
(c) — Basis for depreciation
Status: **MISMATCH**

Restated: Input — Its basis for depreciation: what you paid for the building or improvement, not the land (for a home you lived in before renting it, the smaller of its value and your adjusted basis when you began renting it); your share

Model: The IRS says to "multiply the cost or other basis of the property by the percentage of business/investment use"; the restatement applies no business/investment-use percentage ("your share" reads as an ownership share), which changes the value for partly rented property. The converted-home rule (smaller of value and adjusted basis) and the exclusion of land are not in the text given. Credit/deduction reductions are covered by the screen refusal.

Ruling: open
